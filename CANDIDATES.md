# Lawmaker roast candidates (draft pass)

One satirical sentence each, at most 320 characters, about
lawmakers who regulate AI, crypto or the internet but cannot
convert a file to PDF.

1. Convert the bill: "The committee voted to regulate the
   algorithm, then asked the algorithm's company to convert
   the bill to PDF." -- the dependency is the satire; could
   be sharpened with the hearing-room detail (printed the
   internet, etc.).
2. Printed internet: "Having printed the entire internet for
   the hearing -- 4,000 pages, single-sided -- the senator
   demanded to know why it was so heavy on data." -- visual,
   longer.
3. Password hearing: "The hearing on cybersecurity opened
   with the chair asking IT to read his password aloud,
   slowly, for the record." -- strong single image.
4. PDF amendment: "The amendment to ban deepfakes was itself
   submitted as a photo of a screen, taken at an angle, in
   Word." -- the medium is the joke.
5. Crypto wallet: "He lost the nation's reserve wallet seed
   phrase, then introduced a bill making it illegal to lose
   things." -- broader satire, less PDF-specific.

## Verdict

Winner: candidate 4. The brief's premise is lawmakers who
cannot handle a PDF, and candidate 4 doesn't describe that
incompetence -- it exhibits it, in the artefact itself: an
anti-deepfake amendment filed as an angled photograph of a
screen, in Word. The medium is the entire hearing.
